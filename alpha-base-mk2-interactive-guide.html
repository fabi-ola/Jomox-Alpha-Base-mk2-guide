What the eleven pads actually are

Sound engines

The pad chooses a track; the selected track decides what the 4×4 knob matrix means. The printed black and red labels are alternate parameter maps, not sixteen permanently fixed knobs.

Analog voice · pad 1

Kick Drum

A dedicated analog kick: oscillator tuning, pitch sweep, decay, harmonics, attack/pulse/noise, EQ, compression, metallic noise, gate time and LFO.

Good for: sub bass drums, knocking electro kicks, tuned tom/bass notes.
Analog voice · pad 2

MBrane

Two coupled membrane oscillators. Their pitches, damping and two-way coupling create snares, toms, claves, bells, metallic percussion and even kicks.

Good for: snares that are more than a sample; tune Coupl1/Coupl2 and noise together.
Hybrid analog/sample · pads 3–4

Closed + Open Hi-Hat

A sample and metallic noise feed a specialized analog hi-hat filter, then an analog VCA. LP Cut removes top end, HP Cut removes lows, resonance emphasizes the cutoff, and the VCA envelope shapes length.

REC PARA + encoder 15 toggles choke behavior: an open hat wins over a closed hat on the same step.
Hybrid analog/sample · pads 5–8

Clap, Rim, Crash, Ride

Each mixes a sample with metallic noise, then passes through its own resonance-capable 2-pole multimode analog filter and VCA. The filter can be LP, HP or BP.

With high resonance/self-oscillation these become small synth voices for toms, zaps, claves or filtered bass samples.
Digital sample voices · pads 9–10

X Sample 1 + 2

Pure digital sample tracks with wide pitch range, start/stop, loop, reverse and LFO modulation of pitch, volume and filter target. They can also record external audio into RAM.

Best route for a bass one-shot, vocal hit, texture or melodic sample. Unlike pads 3–8, these do not pass through the dedicated analog filters.
4-operator digital FM · pad 11

FM Synth

A small four-operator, up-to-six-voice FM synth. It can make metallic percussion, basses, leads and chords; an external MIDI keyboard can play it polyphonically.

For the internal sequencer, program FM trigger steps and use SEQ pitch offsets for a melody.

What the RGB LEDs are telling you

Color decoder

Color meaning depends on whether you are looking at the step row or the encoder-matrix LEDs. Brightness and blinking carry meaning too.

Red · programmed stepA normal trigger exists at this position. On encoder LEDs 1–11, blinking red means that instrument is muted.
Orange · accentThe programmed trigger is accented. Hold the instrument pad and press the step to switch red ↔ orange.
Green · playheadThe moving green point is the current sequencer position. It also runs through the 4×4 matrix in pitch-sequence view.
Blue · lock layer 2A step contains a second different parameter edit lock. The display also reports “Step edit lock 2.”
Violet · lock layer 3A third different parameter lock is stored on that step. Accent/non-accent still changes brightness.
Turquoise · recorded track lockSeen when REC PARA records a continuous knob move across the bar; even non-trigger steps can receive locks.
Blinking · special stateContext matters: an encoder blink means mute; a step blink can indicate roll/flam; all 16 flashing means Shift is waiting for a function.
About white or pale blue: the OS 1.0 manual does not define a separate normal “white” step state. A bright turquoise/blue/violet RGB mix can look white in photos or at high brightness. Use the display text (“Step edit lock 1–3”) and whether the light moves or blinks to confirm it.
The operations people most often need

Shift workflows, without guesswork

Press SHIFT once, then the numbered step. Unless noted otherwise, turn DATA to change a value and press ENTER to confirm; SHIFT/CANCEL backs out.

Clear the entire pattern

  1. Select the pattern with EDIT/TRIG off.
  2. Press SHIFT → Step 2.
  3. At “Clear Pattern?” press ENTER.
  4. This clears the temporary pattern buffer; avoid STO PAT if you want the old stored pattern back.

Clear only one sound/track

  1. Turn EDIT/TRIG on and choose the instrument.
  2. For each available BAR A–D, press every lit step to turn it off.
  3. REC PARA + locked step disables/re-enables locks; REC PARA + encoder 12 permanently erases all locks on that track.
  4. The manual does not document a one-command “clear selected track,” so do not use SHIFT+2—it clears the whole pattern.

Make a pattern load its kit

  1. Hold REC PARA and click encoder 16 to enable Preset–Pattern Link.
  2. Load the desired kit with KIT/SEQ → DATA → ENTER.
  3. Select/edit the pattern, then store it with STO PAT twice or SHIFT + 3.
  4. The currently loaded kit is linked when the pattern is stored; recalling the pattern now recalls that kit.

Copy a bar and make a variation

  1. EDIT/TRIG on; choose the track and source BAR.
  2. Press SHIFT → Step 11.
  3. Use BAR to choose destination B/C/D.
  4. Press ENTER, then edit a few steps in the copied bar.

Set track length or polyrhythm

  1. EDIT on + choose track for one-track length; EDIT off for all tracks.
  2. Press SHIFT → Step 5 or LA STEP.
  3. Turn DATA from 1–64 and press ENTER.
  4. BAR exposes each 16-step chunk allowed by that length.

Store vs copy

  1. STO KIT/STO PAT twice quick-stores to the current location.
  2. SHIFT + 1/SHIFT + 3 opens a destination selector.
  3. Turn DATA for another destination, then ENTER to copy/store.
  4. Before the final confirmation, SHIFT/CANCEL aborts.
